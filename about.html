📋 Method & Sources

How this ledger is built.

If you're going to make claims about exams, courts, and powerful institutions, you show your sources. No exceptions. Here is every decision we made — what counts, what doesn't, and why.

🔍 Inclusion criteria

An incident is included only if it was reported by a national or regional newspaper of record, documented in a court order or FIR, cited by a government inquiry, or listed in the Wikipedia master compilation with per-entry citations. Unverified social media claims are excluded. Incidents that remain entirely alleged — with no official action — are tagged "Alleged only."

📅 How dates work

We use the documented date of the exam or the breach — whichever is specifically reported. If only the month and year are in the public record, we default to the 1st of that month. Outcome dates reflect the latest publicly reported information as of September 2026. Some cases took more than a decade to reach conviction.

📎 Sourcing standard

Every case card carries at least one live link to a published source. The dataset uses the Wikipedia master list as a secondary index and, where available, links directly to major reporting or public records. A source link does not by itself mean every allegation is proven; the wording follows the status reported by the cited material. If a link is dead, tell us and we'll update it.

🔢 What "107 means

Researchers have documented 188+ leak incidents across India between 2000 and 2026. This archive currently curates approximately 107 of the most significant and well-sourced cases. The figure "188+" refers to the full documented universe; "107 is what's here. Per-state totals match the documented record. We prioritise depth over completeness.

🏛️ Independence

This archive has no affiliation with any government body, exam agency, coaching institute, or news outlet. No advertising, no sponsorship, nothing for sale. Compiled by one person with assistance from AI research tools. If a source is contested, we label it accordingly — we distinguish between "reported" and "proven."

✏️ Corrections policy

Wrong date? Overstated claim? Broken link? Submit a correction with a verifiable source and we'll fix it immediately. The entire dataset lives in assets/data.js. Corrections are one-line changes. Write to sahilpsycheofficial@gmail.com with the subject line "Correction — [incident name]".

⚠️ Read before citing

All data on this site is compiled from third-party published sources, linked on every entry. It exists for public interest and research — not as an official court record. Summaries compress complex cases; always click through to the original before quoting. Allegations are labelled as such. "Documented" means publicly reported by a verifiable source, not proven beyond reasonable doubt in a court.
