📜 The Long Story

Twenty-four years. Four eras. One broken system.

Over two decades, the mechanics of examination malpractice evolved from analog printing-press thefts to encrypted Darknet broadcasts. Through changing technologies, political regimes, and legislative reforms, the structural vulnerability of India's testing pipeline remained constant. This is the chronological record.

2002–09 The Quiet Years

Before smartphones, leaks required legwork. Papers were physically lifted from strongrooms, printed at press facilities, and distributed through tout networks that operated like well-oiled courier companies. CBI investigations moved slowly — some cases from 2002 only yielded convictions in 2024–25. The victims: AIIMS PG aspirants, IIM candidates, railway recruitment hopefuls, and CBSE medical students, among others.

2010–16 The Vyapam Machine

Madhya Pradesh's Vyapam board turned exam fraud into an organised industry. Roll numbers were rigged, proxy solvers were seated next to beneficiaries, forged certificates enabled impersonators to sit exams. The scandal spanned medical admissions, police recruitment, food inspectors, contract teachers — virtually every exam the board conducted between 2008 and 2013. Ministers were named in FIRs. Witnesses died under unexplained circumstances. The Supreme Court transferred all cases to the CBI in 2015. Meanwhile, AIEEE and other national exams were discovering that digital tools only made leaking faster.

2017–23 The WhatsApp Era

One forwarded photo. Ten lakh candidates' futures on the line. In 2017, Bihar's BSSC clerical exam was leaked on WhatsApp — and an IAS officer-turned-chairman went to jail. SSC shut down pen-and-paper MTS examinations entirely. Rajasthan ran through teacher recruitment, police recruitment, and sub-inspector exams in a cascade of leaks between 2021 and 2023, eventually passing one of India's strictest anti-leak laws: 10 years jail and ₹10 crore fine. Telangana's TSPSC servers were found dumping papers to the darknet. Online exams faced remote-access attacks. The medium had changed; the system had not.

2024–26 The Reckoning

May 2024: CBI reconstructed how a paper left a Hazaribagh strongroom through a torn seal, a lighter, and a toolkit — and reached Patna candidates for ₹30–50 lakh. The Supreme Court confirmed 155 beneficiaries but kept the exam standing. UGC-NET was cancelled the day after it was held — paper had been on the darknet. The government fast-tracked the Public Examinations Act. Two years later, NEET-UG 2026 was cancelled mid-session when a "guess paper" was reported to match 120 of 180 questions. Re-NEET arrived via IAF aircraft. Twelve aspirants were reported to have died by suicide. Ten thousand students marched to Parliament. The Education Minister was reported to have resigned on 25 July 2026.
